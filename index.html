Rainbow subgraphs: obstructions, progress and open problems

A survey of rainbow matchings, trees, paths, cycles and spanning structures in edge-coloured graphs, digraphs and hypergraphs: from Euler's thirty-six officers to results of 2025 and 2026.

Start reading Download the PDF

Abstract

A subgraph of an edge-coloured graph is rainbow if its edges have distinct colours. Questions of this kind go back to Euler's thirty-six officers and to transversals in Latin squares, and they now connect graph labellings, matroid bases, group orderings, additive combinatorics, coding theory, design theory and fair division. We survey rainbow matchings, trees, paths, cycles and general spanning structures, and their analogues for digraphs and hypergraphs. For each structure we ask when a rainbow copy exists, how many disjoint copies there are and when the host decomposes into them, under proper, locally bounded and globally bounded colourings and colour-degree conditions, for graph collections and in random settings. Recent results include the Ryser–Brualdi–Stein bound $n-1$ for large Latin squares, Ringel's tree packing conjecture and Andersen's rainbow path conjecture for large $n$, and asymptotic forms of Rota's basis conjecture. For each topic we describe the constructions that limit what can be true and the problems that remain open. Anti-Ramsey numbers, rainbow Turán numbers and rainbow connection are not covered.
